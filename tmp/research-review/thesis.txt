ABSTRACT
Elderly residential care homes and assisted-living facilities in Sri Lanka face
operational challenges characterized by acute caregiver shortages, paper-dependent
medical recordkeeping, and passive monitoring. These deficiencies can lead to
adverse events, including missed prescription dosages and undetected emotional
distress. This dissertation presents the Machine Learning Smart Prescription
Reader and Caretaker Assistant System (implemented as the CareConnect
platform), an integrated, privacy-preserving healthcare management, automated
prescription interpretation, and smart caretaking ecosystem engineered specifically for
resource-constrained institutional care facilities.
The research addresses four core objectives: (1) multimodal emotion
recognition for alerting caregivers to resident psychological distress, (2) automated
machine learning prescription interpretation and dosage schedule reminders for
patients, (3) personalized dietary notification management, and (4) real-time smart
alert dispatching to elevate caretaker responsiveness. To protect resident dignity, the
system implements an on-device, edge-first affective telemetry engine utilizing face-
api.js (68-point facial landmark analysis) and the Web Audio API for vocal frequency
profiling, executing client-side without transmitting raw video or audio over
networks. Simultaneously, an ensemble OCR pipeline integrates a custom fine-tuned
Swin-BART Document Understanding Transformer (Donut) model, Google Gemini
2.5 Flash multimodal intelligence, and regular expression parsing backed by an
RxNorm pharmacology knowledge base. All OCR outputs require human caretaker
verification prior to medication administration.
Quantitative evaluation on medical prescription benchmarks demonstrated that
the ensemble OCR achieved an F1-score of 0.932 (Character Error Rate of 0.038).
The edge emotion engine attained 88.5% classification accuracy across affective
states with an average processing latency under 120 milliseconds. User Acceptance
Testing across 24 system stakeholders yielded an average System Usability Scale
(SUS) score of 85.1, confirming high operational feasibility. Future work should focus
on extending the ensemble OCR pipeline to support handwritten Sinhala and Tamil
medical scripts, expanding nocturnal vital sign tracking, and integrating multi-drug
interaction screening.
Keywords: Prescription Reader, Caretaker Assistant System, Ambient Assisted
Living, Affective Computing.

CHAPTER 1
INTRODUCTION
1.1 Background of the Study
As the global elderly population rises, especially in developing countries like
Sri Lanka, the need for intelligent caregiving solutions has become increasingly
urgent. According to demographic projections published by the World Health
Organization (WHO), the global population aged 60 years and older is anticipated to
surpass 2.1 billion by 2050. Within South Asia, Sri Lanka possesses one of the most
rapidly aging populations, where individuals aged 60 and above will constitute over
28% of the total populace by 2040. Traditional care models in elderly orphanages and
charitable nursing facilities are often under-resourced and lack the capacity for
continuous monitoring, which frequently leads to delayed responses to acute health
emergencies or emotional distress. The integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and
computer vision into elder care presents a transformative opportunity to enhance
resident safety, provide timely clinical interventions, and elevate the overall quality of
institutional life.
AI-based monitoring and computer vision systems have been widely
investigated for patient and elderly care. Foundational studies, such as the
investigation by Chen et al. [11], explored how AI-powered vision systems can
monitor physical movements to detect falls, wandering, or abnormal behaviors among
elderly cohorts. Similarly, the work of Nasrallah et al. [12] demonstrated how smart
cameras combined with object detection algorithms can interpret daily activities in
assisted living facilities, substantially reducing the risks of unattended incidents. In
parallel, emotion detection through computer vision has emerged as a cornerstone for
geriatric mental health assessment. Grounded in Ekman's [28] foundational theory of
facial expressions, recent deep learning advancements synthesized by Li and Deng
[27] establish that convolutional and recurrent neural networks (such as CNN-LSTM
architectures) can classify emotional states in real time from facial expressions,
providing critical telemetry for detecting silent psychological distress in
institutionalized seniors.
Simultaneously, medical alert systems and automated health telemetry have
evolved significantly in modern clinical informatics. Ahmed et al. [6] investigated
IoT-based real-time health monitoring using wearable sensor nodes to track vital signs
such as heart rate and skin temperature; however, in low-resource institutional care,

PAGE 19
non-invasive visual and data-driven detection offers a far more sustainable and
dignified alternative that eliminates the burden of wearable charging and skin
irritation. Furthermore, integrating Electronic Health Records (EHR) with automated
medication reminder workflows was highlighted by Kruse et al. [14] as essential for
mitigating transcription errors and enforcing compliance. In ambient surveillance,
Bhattacharya et al. [13] emphasized that ethical AI and privacy-preserving data
architectures are mandatory prerequisites when deploying monitoring technologies in
vulnerable healthcare environments, advocating for client-edge processing and data
anonymization. Moreover, empirical trials by Wen et al. [22] demonstrated that
deploying smart real-time patient alerting systems routed directly to mobile devices
reduced caregiver response latencies by over 50% (from 145.7 seconds to 59.0
seconds), underscoring the vital need for automated caretaker-assistance tools in
understaffed care settings.
Despite these promising advancements across individual research vectors,
there is limited research combining all these elements—machine learning-based
handwritten prescription parsing, affective state analysis, personalized dietary
tracking, and real-time alert generation—into a single, unified, cost-effective system
tailored for resource-limited elderly care institutions. The Machine Learning Smart
Prescription Reader and Caretaker Assistant System (CareConnect) bridges this
critical empirical and technological gap.
1.2 Research Problem Statement
Orphanages and old age homes in Sri Lanka certainly serve a useful purpose,
but many of them operate with skeleton staff as well as outdated administrative
oversight systems. This leads to the repeated occurrence of critical oversights such as
missed medication dosages, emotional distress, or health crises. Current caregiving
practices lack the capability to adapt smartly and in the moment. This study aims to
design an advanced AI-powered monitoring system that actively tracks and analyses
residents' behavioural, emotional, and health data to enhance care responsiveness and
overall standard. The goal of this research is to address the gap in intelligent
caregiving systems for the elderly by providing practical innovative technology-based
solutions designed specifically for underfunded elderly care facilities.
Specifically, the operational realities within these facilities expose four acute,
systemic problems:
1. Severe Illegibility and Vulnerability of Handwritten Prescriptions:
Clinical prescriptions issued by regional medical officers and visiting
physicians are written by hand on paper slips exhibiting cursive degradation,

PAGE 20
ink smudges, and non-standard dosage abbreviations. Traditional optical
character recognition (OCR) engines fail when confronted with handwritten
medical scripts, achieving unusable word accuracy. Overburdened caretakers
are forced to interpret cursive notations manually under time pressure,
introducing high risks of transcription errors, wrong dosages, or omitted
pharmaceuticals.
2. Absence of Continuous Affective Distress and Wellness Telemetry: Elderly
institutional residents suffering from depression, loneliness, medication-
induced dizziness, or emerging physiological distress frequently do not self-
report symptoms. Periodic, manual check-ins conducted by skeleton
caretaking staff fail to detect transient emotional anomalies or affective
declines occurring between rounds.
3. Nutritional Oversights and Lack of Personalized Dietary Management:
Geriatric residents commonly suffer from chronic comorbidities (e.g., diabetes
mellitus, hypertension, chronic kidney disease) requiring strict dietary
modifications. Communal meal preparation in elder homes lacks automated
nutritional tracking, calorie calculation, and real-time dietary warning
notifications for contra-indicated food items.
4. Fragmented Administrative Workflows and Caregiver Fatigue: Caretaker
teams lack a centralized digital management environment uniting resident
medical profiles, prescription reminders, emergency SOS dispatches, and shift
scheduling. Fragmented paper ledgers generate administrative friction,
miscommunication, and delayed response times during resident health crises.
1.3 Research Questions
In direct conformity with Section 5 of the approved Undergraduate Research
Proposal Form, this study is structured to investigate and answer the following four
core research questions:
1. Research Question 1: How accurately can a computer vision-based model
extract and interpret handwritten or printed prescriptions to ensure correct
medication identification and dosage schedules?
2. Research Question 2: Which OCR (Optical Character Recognition) and NLP
techniques are most effective for prescription analysis in diverse handwriting
formats and multi-language (Tamil/Sinhala/English) contexts?
3. Research Question 3: What are the challenges in integrating a smart
prescription reader with real-time patient alert systems in underfunded

PAGE 21
orphanages and elderly care facilities?
4. Research Question 4: Can the integration of prescription reading with dietary
and emotional alert modules enhance medication adherence and overall patient
care in low-resource caregiving environments?
1.4 Aim(s) and Objectives of the Project
1.4.1 Research Aim
To design and develop an AI-powered smart monitoring and alert system using
computer vision and personalized health-emotion data to enhance the safety, well-
being, and real-time care of elderly orphanage residents. The system will identify
behavior patterns, monitor emotional and health-related anomalies, and give timely
alerts to caretakers, thus surpassing the limitations of traditional caregiving methods
and maximizing total care efficiency in low-resource environments.
1.4.2 Specific Objectives
In direct alignment with Section 4 of the approved Undergraduate Research
Proposal, the overarching research aim is accomplished through the execution of four
specific, measurable, and technically verifiable objectives:
1. Objective 1 — Emotion Recognition for Alerting the Caregiver: To apply
computer vision and client-side machine learning techniques to recognize
facial expressions and vocal cues indicative of emotional distress, depression,
or discomfort in elderly residents, generating immediate wellness telemetry to
alert on-duty caregivers without invading resident privacy.
2. Objective 2 — Prescription Reminders to the Patient: To design, train, and
orchestrate an ensemble machine learning and OCR architecture capable of
reading handwritten clinical prescriptions, extracting structured dosage
regimens, and generating automated, timely prescription reminders directly to
patients and caregivers to enforce pharmacological compliance.
3. Objective 3 — To Provide Personalized Dietary Notifications: To formulate
and deploy a personalized nutrition and dietary management engine that
analyzes individual resident health profiles, calculates caloric and
macronutrient targets, and delivers automated dietary notifications and meal
plans adapted to chronic geriatric conditions.
4. Objective 4 — To Enhance Caregiver Responsiveness Through Smart
Alerts: To design and implement a centralized smart alert and notification

PAGE 22
mechanism that enables caregivers to receive and respond to resident SOS
emergencies, missed medication notifications, and emotional distress alerts
efficiently.
1.5 Motivation and Significance of the Project
The motivation underpinning this research is rooted in the humanitarian
imperative to elevate the standard of living, safety, and psychological dignity of
elderly residents residing in institutional care homes across Sri Lanka and comparable
developing territories. As filial care networks erode under economic and migratory
pressures, the elder orphanage sector serves as the final social safety net for
vulnerable citizens. Enhancing the quality and efficiency of care within these
institutions without imposing unsustainable capital expenses represents a pressing
societal priority.
The academic and technical significance of this study manifests across four
distinct axes:
Ensemble Deep Learning for Degraded Document Intelligence: Rather
than relying on a solitary machine learning model, this study contributes an
ensemble architecture integrating an end-to-end Swin Transformer and BART-
based Document Understanding Transformer (Donut) model, Google Gemini
2.5 Flash multimodal intelligence, and deterministic regular expression
parsing backed by an RxNorm pharmacology database. This ensemble
paradigm establishes how specialized vision-language transformers can be
harmonized with large multimodal reasoning to overcome extreme
handwriting variability.
Privacy-Preserving Multimodal Edge Affective Computing: The system
demonstrates that meaningful mental health and wellness telemetry can be
captured directly at the browser client edge using WebAssembly-accelerated
deep learning (face-api.js) and the Web Audio API without streaming raw
video or audio data across networks. This edge-first paradigm helps mitigate
privacy and dignity concerns associated with continuous monitoring.
Holistic Tripartite Healthcare Management Ecosystem: Unlike narrow
point solutions that address solely computer vision or database administration
in isolation, CareConnect synthesizes a comprehensive ecosystem featuring
tailored portals for Patients, Caretakers, and System Administrators, coupled
with automated nutrition planning and real-time emergency dispatching.

PAGE 23
Socio-Economic Feasibility for Underfunded Institutions: The entire
software framework is engineered leveraging open-source technologies (React
18, Node.js, MySQL, Python PyTorch, Vite), edge client inference, and
standard consumer hardware (laptops, mobile webcams, integrated
microphones). This architecture eliminates high licensing fees and costly
specialized biometric hardware, making deployment practically viable for
resource-limited non-governmental organizations (NGOs) and charitable elder
homes.
1.6 Limitations of the Project
To preserve scientific transparency and academic rigor, several genuine
technical and operational limitations of the developed system are explicitly
recognized:
Prescription Handwriting Degradation Boundaries: Although the ensemble
OCR architecture significantly outperforms classical OCR, extreme instances
of physical paper degradation (e.g., severe fluid staining, torn document
fragments, overlapping ink bleed-through) or severely non-standard doctor
shorthand remain challenging, necessitating mandatory human-in-the-loop
validation before clinical dispensation.
Acoustic and Optical Environmental Sensitivity: Edge facial landmark
detection requires adequate ambient illumination and an unobstructed facial
view within ±30 degrees of yaw. Similarly, vocal tone feature extraction is
susceptible to high ambient background noise common in communal dining
halls or shared wards, which may temporarily distort acoustic spectral
features.
Dependence on Cloud Connectivity for Secondary AI Inference: While the
core facial and vocal emotion engine and relational database operate locally on
the facility's local area network (LAN), the secondary verification step of the
prescription ensemble utilizes the Google Gemini API, requiring an active
internet connection. Offline operation gracefully falls back to local Donut and
regex parsing.
Scope of Non-Invasive Sensing: The system intentionally restricts its
wellness tracking to non-invasive affective telemetry (facial expression and
vocal tone) and does not measure direct hemodynamic vitals (such as invasive
arterial blood pressure, continuous ECG, or blood glucose levels), which
require specialized medical diagnostic equipment.

PAGE 24
Dataset Language Specificity: The prescription OCR model and RxNorm
pharmacology dictionary are optimized for English medical terminology, Latin
dosage abbreviations, and standard brand/generic drug naming conventions
prevalent in Sri Lankan medical institutions, and do not currently support
vernacular Sinhala or Tamil handwritten scripts.

LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1 Introduction
The synthesis of artificial intelligence, ambient sensing, and biomedical
informatics within geriatric residential care occupies an increasingly vital position in
contemporary computing research. To establish a rigorous theoretical and empirical
foundation for the CareConnect ecosystem, this chapter conducts a critical, systematic
review of foundational and state-of-the-art literature across four primary technological
domains: Ambient Assisted Living (AAL) frameworks, optical character recognition
(OCR) and vision-language transformers applied to clinical prescriptions, facial and
acoustic affective computing, and privacy-preserving edge computing architectures.
The chapter culminates in a structured comparative evaluation of existing commercial
and academic systems, highlighting critical research gaps that the CareConnect
architecture explicitly addresses.
2.2 Ambient Assisted Living and Smart Geriatric Healthcare
Ambient Assisted Living (AAL) encompasses an interdisciplinary paradigm
combining pervasive sensing, artificial intelligence, and human-computer interaction
to foster independence, safety, and health management for aging populations [1].
Initial generations of AAL systems relied predominantly on environmental binary
sensors, such as passive infrared (PIR) motion detectors, acoustic door contact
sensors, and pressure-sensitive floor mats [2], [3]. While non-intrusive and
computationally inexpensive, these early environmental systems suffer from an acute
inability to capture fine-grained behavioral semantics or assess physiological and
emotional nuances; they can detect whether a resident entered a room, but cannot
discern whether the resident is in acute physical agony, taking their prescribed
medication, or weeping from severe psychological distress [4].
To overcome the contextual blindness of environmental sensors, subsequent
research pivoted toward wearable Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) devices [5].
Ahmed et al. [6] proposed an IoT-based tele-monitoring framework utilizing wrist-
worn sensor nodes integrating photoplethysmography (PPG) and tri-axial
accelerometers to stream continuous heart rate and kinetic telemetry to clinical
dashboards. Similarly, Francis and Babu [7] demonstrated wearable sensor networks

PAGE 27
for real-time arrhythmia and fall detection in residential patient cohorts. However,
empirical investigations into the deployment of wearable technology within geriatric
institutional populations consistently document high rates of abandonment and
operational failure [8]. As Rashidi and Mihailidis [9] observed, elderly residents,
particularly those presenting mild cognitive impairment (MCI) or dementia,
frequently forget to recharge wearable batteries, misplace the devices, or actively
remove them due to tactile hypersensitivity, skin irritation, or cognitive disorientation.
These persistent operational limitations prompted computer vision researchers
to explore non-contact, camera-based ambient monitoring [10]. Chen et al. [11]
investigated deep learning vision sensors deployed in assisted-living facilities,
utilizing Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) to classify posture sequences for
automated fall detection and wandering behavior tracking. Nasrallah et al. [12]
implemented real-time activity recognition utilizing object detection networks in
smart care facilities, demonstrating that ambient visual monitoring significantly
reduced response times to unattended physical incidents. Nevertheless, traditional
vision-based monitoring introduces profound ethical and psychological barriers.
Constant visual surveillance within residential living quarters instills severe feelings
of dignity loss, psychological anxiety, and perceived violation of personal autonomy
among residents [13]. Consequently, contemporary ambient care research demands
intelligent systems that deliver automated clinical oversight without resorting to
intrusive video streaming or burdensome wearable peripherals.
2.3 Medical Prescription Recognition and Clinical OCR
Pharmacological management represents the most safety-critical daily
operational process within institutional eldercare. Adverse drug events (ADEs)
attributable to transcription errors, dosage misinterpretations, and delayed medication
administration constitute a major source of preventable morbidity in geriatric care
[14]. Automating the transcription of clinical prescriptions has consequently remained
a vital yet persistently challenging objective within biomedical engineering.
2.3.1 Classical OCR Pipelines and CRNN Architectures
Traditional document digitization pipelines rely heavily on multi-stage optical
character recognition frameworks, such as Tesseract OCR or modular Convolutional
Recurrent Neural Networks (CRNN) paired with Connectionist Temporal
Classification (CTC) loss [15], [16]. A classical CRNN pipeline operates in sequential
stages:

PAGE 28
1. Binarization and morphological preprocessing to eliminate paper noise and
uneven illumination;
2. Line and word-level text segmentation using horizontal and vertical projection
profiles or contour bounding;
3. Feature extraction across isolated character sequences utilizing alternating
convolutional layers;
4. Sequence modeling using Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM)
recurrent layers; and
5. Transcription decoding utilizing CTC beam search to map frame predictions to
linguistic tokens.
While CRNN architectures demonstrate robust performance on clean, printed,
machine-typeset documents, their efficacy collapses when confronted with
handwritten clinical prescriptions [17]. Handwritten clinical scripts exhibit extreme
stroke variability, cursive ligatures, irregular baseline slants, overlapping lines, and
ink bleed-through. Crucially, classical pipelines suffer from severe error
compounding: if the initial morphological text segmentation incorrectly segments a
cursive pharmaceutical name (e.g., segmenting the ligature between "Met-" and "-
formin"), downstream recurrent layers and CTC decoders fail catastrophically without
recovery mechanisms [18].
2.3.2 End-to-End Vision Transformers: The Donut Architecture
To transcend the fragility of multi-stage OCR pipelines, recent breakthroughs
in deep learning have introduced end-to-end vision-language models that bypass
intermediate text segmentation entirely. Prominent among these is the Donut
(Document Understanding Transformer) architecture introduced by Kim et al. [19].
Donut reformulates visual document understanding as a direct image-to-sequence
translation task, mapping raw document pixels directly into structured text or JSON
syntax without relying on external off-the-shelf OCR engines.
The Donut model couples a hierarchical Shifted Window (Swin) Transformer
visual encoder with a multilingual Bidirectional and Auto-Regressive Transformer
(BART) textual decoder [20], [21]. The Swin Transformer processes high-resolution
document images by computing self-attention within local non-overlapping windows
and allowing cross-window connections via shifted window partitioning, achieving
linear computational complexity relative to image dimensions. The BART decoder
then attends to the high-level visual representations and auto-regressively generates
desired linguistic tokens guided by task-specific prompts (such as <s_ocr>). By

PAGE 29
learning character recognition, language modeling, and document layout
understanding jointly within a single differentiable neural network, Donut
demonstrates exceptional resilience against cursive distortions, erratic baselines, and
degraded paper substrates that cause classical OCR to fail [19].
2.3.3 Large Multimodal Models (Gemini Vision) in Medicine
The advent of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), exemplified by Google
Gemini 2.5 Flash, has introduced unprecedented reasoning capabilities into medical
informatics [23]. Unlike narrow OCR networks trained purely on pixel-to-character
alignments, multimodal foundational models possess vast contextual knowledge
encompassing clinical pharmacology, international drug nomenclature, dosage forms,
and medical abbreviations [24]. When presented with an ambiguous handwritten
glyph that could visually represent either "Clonazepam" or "Clozapine," an LMM can
evaluate contextual medical cues—such as the surrounding diagnostic notes,
accompanying anti-hypertensive therapies, or standard clinical dosage magnitudes—
to resolve the visual ambiguity with high clinical plausibility [25].
However, deploying foundational cloud LMMs as solitary transcription
engines introduces distinct engineering challenges: cloud API inference incurs
substantial round-trip network latency, introduces potential token costs that strain
charitable budgets, and mandates fallback strategies during wide-area network
outages [26]. Consequently, an architectural consensus is emerging that advocates for
hybrid ensemble architectures, wherein specialized local vision transformers execute
primary transcription while auxiliary cloud multimodal reasoning cross-validates
ambiguous entities and enforces pharmacological consistency [27].
2.4 Affective Computing and Emotion Recognition in Eldercare
Affective computing, pioneered theoretically by Picard [28], seeks to develop
computational systems capable of recognizing, interpreting, and synthesizing human
emotional states. In geriatric care, continuous affective assessment provides an
objective, non-verbal telemetry channel into resident wellness, serving as an early
indicator of depressive episodes, severe anxiety, or adverse drug side-effects such as
nausea and dizziness [29].
2.4.1 Facial Expression Recognition and Landmark Geometry
Facial Expression Recognition (FER) predominantly grounds its taxonomy in
Ekman’s foundational Facial Action Coding System (FACS), which decomposes
human emotional expressions into discrete Action Units (AUs) corresponding to

PAGE 30
specific facial muscle movements [30]. In modern computer vision, FER pipelines
deploy convolutional neural networks or lightweight mobile backbones (such as
MobileNet-SSD) to detect faces, extract 68 dense facial landmark coordinates
(delineating eyebrows, orbital margins, nasal bridge, and oral contours), and classify
affective expressions across primary valence categories: happiness, sadness, anger,
fear, disgust, surprise, and neutrality [31].
In client-side web architectures, libraries such as face-api.js execute quantized
neural models directly inside the browser runtime leveraging WebAssembly (WASM)
and WebGL hardware acceleration [32]. This capability is critical for healthcare
environments: because inference occurs locally on the client device's GPU, video
frames captured from the local camera are never serialized or transmitted across a
network socket, significantly reducing privacy exposure by processing raw
audiovisual data locally and maintaining compliance with international healthcare
data governance standards [33].
2.4.2 Speech Emotion Recognition and Acoustic Features
While facial expressions provide high affective resolution, they are inherently
prone to occlusion when a resident turns away from the camera or exhibits a blunted,
masked facial affect common in Parkinson’s disease or post-stroke paralysis [34].
Consequently, Speech Emotion Recognition (SER) provides a vital complementary
telemetry modality. Psychoacoustic research demonstrates that emotional states
modulate autonomic nervous system activity, directly altering vocal cord tension,
subglottal respiratory pressure, and vocal tract articulation [35].
Standard acoustic feature extraction frameworks capture these autonomic
shifts across three primary dimensions:
Prosodic Features: Fundamental frequency (F0 / pitch), pitch contour
variability, and root-mean-square (RMS) acoustic energy;
Spectral Features: Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs), spectral
centroid, and spectral roll-off, which reflect vocal tract resonance and timbre;
and
Temporal Features: Speech rate, zero-crossing rate, and pause-to-
vocalization duration ratios [36].
Elevated vocal pitch variability and rapid energy spikes strongly correlate with
acute panic or distress, whereas reduced pitch contour, low acoustic energy, and
elongated pauses signify depressive withdrawal or physical fatigue [37].

PAGE 31
2.4.3 Multimodal Affective Fusion Paradigms
Unimodal affective systems—relying solely on facial or vocal data—exhibit
substantial error rates when confronted with real-world sensory degradations, such as
poor room lighting or acoustic reverberation. To achieve robust classification,
affective computing utilizes multimodal fusion, conventionally categorized into:
Early (Feature-Level) Fusion: Concatenating raw visual landmark feature
vectors with acoustic spectral vectors prior to classification; and
Late (Decision-Level) Fusion: Processing visual and acoustic streams
through separate specialized classification models and subsequently
combining their posterior probability distributions using weighted averaging,
Bayesian inference, or fuzzy logic [38].
Late decision-level fusion presents distinct architectural superiority for
distributed healthcare applications. It allows individual sensory pipelines to operate
asynchronously at native sampling rates (e.g., 30 FPS for visual streams versus 16
kHz for audio buffers) and exhibits robust fault tolerance: if the visual camera is
temporarily occluded, the acoustic classifier continues functioning independently,
preventing total system downtime [39].
2.5 Edge Computing and Privacy-Preserving Healthcare
Architectures
The deployment of pervasive artificial intelligence within institutional
eldercare intersects directly with stringent ethical and data protection mandates,
including the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the Sri Lanka Personal
Data Protection Act No. 9 of 2022 [40]. Traditional healthcare IoT deployments adopt
a cloud-centric topology, wherein continuous telemetry is uploaded to remote data
centers for centralized deep learning inference [41]. This topology introduces severe
vulnerabilities:
Severe Privacy and Surveillance Risks: Streaming continuous high-
definition video of elderly residents in vulnerable living conditions creates
catastrophic liability in the event of cloud server breach or unauthorized
interception;
Bandwidth Saturation: Transmitting uncompressed concurrent video and
audio feeds from dozens of residents quickly overwhelms standard
commercial broadband connections available in charitable facilities; and

PAGE 32
High Operational Latency: Remote network round-trips introduce
unpredictable delays, impairing rapid dispatch of emergency alerts during
acute clinical crises [42].
Edge computing resolves these systemic vulnerabilities by relocating artificial
intelligence inference directly to the periphery of the network—on local gateways,
workstations, or client browser runtimes [43]. By processing raw sensory signals
locally and transmitting solely structured, non-identifying telemetry (such as a scalar
wellness index or parsed medication JSON object), edge-first architectures reduce the
exposure of sensitive raw data, eliminate recurring cloud bandwidth expenditures, and
ensure sub-second alerting responsiveness [44].

2.7 Research Gaps Identified
The critical evaluation of existing academic literature and commercial
implementations reveals four profound research and architectural gaps:
1. Gap 1: Absence of Integrated Multimodal Healthcare Ecosystems in
Eldercare: The prevailing literature remains heavily siloed. Computer vision
researchers focus exclusively on isolated benchmarks (such as fall detection or
facial emotion classification on curated datasets), whereas health informatics
researchers focus on database architectures or electronic health records. There
is a glaring absence of integrated, production-grade systems that synthesize
intelligent document digitization, continuous affective wellness monitoring,
and operational administrative workflows into a single cohesive, accessible
platform tailored for institutional caregiving.

PAGE 35
2. Gap 2: Inability of Solitary OCR Engines to Handle Degraded Clinical
Handwriting: Existing clinical digitization systems either require clean
machine-printed electronic prescriptions or rely on single OCR models that
fail on unstructured, cursive doctor handwriting. Literature lacks an empirical
evaluation of an asynchronous ensemble pipeline that fuses fine-tuned vision-
language transformers (Donut) with large multimodal models (Gemini) and
deterministic pharmacological dictionaries (RxNorm) through confidence-
weighted voting.
3. Gap 3: Ethical Impasse Between Eldercare Surveillance and Personal
Dignity: Prior ambient vision monitoring frameworks stream continuous
video to central servers, provoking severe dignity and privacy objections from
residents and guardians. Conversely, systems avoiding cameras rely on
wearable sensors that suffer from non-compliance. There is a critical research
gap in deploying client-side, in-browser edge AI that computes composite
wellness indices locally without transmitting raw media streams across
network boundaries.
4. Gap 4: Neglect of Resource-Constrained Institutional Realities in the
Global South: State-of-the-art AAL systems documented in literature assume
high-speed fiber connectivity, costly specialized hardware, and technically
proficient clinical nursing staff. These systems are economically and
operationally non-viable in underfunded charitable elder homes and
orphanages in developing nations such as Sri Lanka, where software must
execute on commodity hardware, maintain offline resilience, and
accommodate non-technical caregivers.
2.8 Chapter Summary
This chapter provided a rigorous, systematic review of foundational and
contemporary literature governing ambient assisted living, clinical document
intelligence, affective computing, and privacy-preserving edge architectures. The
analysis demonstrated the acute limitations of classical OCR when processing cursive
handwritten prescriptions, established the theoretical advantages of the end-to-end
Donut transformer and Gemini multimodal reasoning, and justified late decision-level
multimodal fusion for facial and vocal affective tracking. Furthermore, the
comparative evaluation synthesized four critical research gaps: the lack of holistic
integration, the vulnerability of solitary OCR models, the privacy dilemma of video
surveillance, and the socio-economic mismatch of existing AAL technologies in
developing regions. These gaps directly inform the architectural, functional, and

PAGE 36
methodological design of CareConnect, detailed comprehensively in subsequent
chapters.

3.1 Introduction
System analysis establishes the empirical, functional, and operational bedrock
of the software engineering lifecycle. Prior to formulating architectural abstractions or
committing source code, it is imperative to rigorously dissect the operational context,
resource limitations, user behaviors, and domain challenges prevalent within
residential geriatric care facilities. This chapter presents an exhaustive requirements
analysis for the CareConnect ecosystem. It commences with a four-dimensional
feasibility study evaluating the project's technical, operational, economic, and ethical

non-functional requirements, presents a comprehensive ethical compliance

3.2 Feasibility Study
To verify that CareConnect is practically realizable within the resource
parameters of an undergraduate research initiative and sustainable when deployed in
charitable elder homes, a formal feasibility assessment was conducted across four

3.2.1 Technical Feasibility
The technical feasibility evaluation addressed three central engineering
questions:
Can modern deep learning transformer architectures (Donut) and large
multimodal models (Gemini) be successfully trained, fine-tuned, and
orchestrated to decipher handwritten clinical prescriptions with high accuracy?
Can real-time facial expression tracking and vocal tone feature extraction
execute fluidly on client commodity hardware inside modern web browsers
without server reliance?

3.2.2 Operational Feasibility
Operational feasibility evaluates whether the intended users—primarily non-
technical elderly residents, overburdened caregivers, and facility administrative
officers—can seamlessly assimilate CareConnect into daily operational routines
without extensive training or friction.
CareConnect addresses operational feasibility through extreme user-centric
simplification:

PAGE 40
Zero-Burden Resident Wellness Logging: Elderly residents are not required
to navigate complex menus or complete psychological surveys. They simply
initiate a 5-second wellness check where the webcam and microphone
passively capture affective cues;
Bedside Prescription Digitization: Caretakers capture a photograph of
handwritten prescriptions using a smartphone camera or tablet, receiving
structured medication suggestions within seconds for one-click verification;
and
Role-Differentiated Interfaces: Three distinct portals ensure that users
encounter only relevant operational tools, completely insulating elderly users
from administrative complexity.

3.3.2 Field Observations in Eldercare Institutions
Passive observational audits were conducted across two residential care
facilities over three separate site visits. Observers shadowed caregivers during
morning medication rounds (07:00–09:00), midday meal administration (12:00–
13:30), and evening shift turnovers (18:00–19:30). The audits revealed that caregivers
spent an average of 42 minutes per shift manually cross-referencing prescription
paper slips against medicine blister packs, representing a massive operational time
drain. Furthermore, ambient lighting conditions in resident wards varied drastically
between daylight and dim nocturnal illumination, underscoring that computer vision
algorithms must operate robustly across dynamic illumination profiles.
3.3.3 Clinical Prescription Document Sampling
A representative audit sample of 120 historical, anonymized paper
prescriptions was examined with institutional permission. Analysis revealed
significant variability in handwriting slant, varying ink pigmentation (ballpoint pen
versus gel ink), paper degradation (creasing, yellowing, water stains), and extensive
utilization of Latin dosage shorthand (om, bd, tds, qds, prn, stat). This empirical
evidence confirmed that off-the-shelf OCR engines (such as standard Tesseract)
produce word error rates exceeding 65% on clinical scripts, mandating a specialized
transformer-based ensemble architecture.
3.3.4 Stakeholder Questionnaires
A structured questionnaire incorporating Keller’s ARCS (Attention,
Relevance, Confidence, Satisfaction) model of motivational design and the System
Usability Scale (SUS) framework was administered to 12 cognitively intact elderly
residents and 8 caregivers. The findings demonstrated a strong demand for high-
contrast, large-button user interfaces, audio confirmation of completed actions, and
explicit reassurance that private video would not be uploaded to the internet.

3.7 Ethical Considerations
Because this research and development project involves AI-driven monitoring
of elderly individuals and the handling of sensitive personal healthcare data, strict
ethical protocols were established in direct compliance with Section 9 of the approved
Research Proposal Form:
1. Privacy and Surveillance Concerns: The use of camera-based AI systems
raises acute concerns about constant surveillance in care environments.
Elderly individuals may experience psychological discomfort, distress, or
perceived loss of dignity due to being monitored. CareConnect prioritizes non-
intrusive monitoring by executing facial and vocal feature extraction directly
on client device memory (WASM/WebGL), strictly forbidding the
transmission or persistence of raw video frames or audio recordings.
2. Informed Consent and Participant Rights: Elderly residents (or their
designated legal guardians in cases of advanced cognitive impairment) must
provide voluntary, informed consent prior to participating in wellness
assessments. Plain-language participant information sheets and verbal
explanations in Sinhala, Tamil, and English were provided, clarifying what
data is collected, how it is processed, and confirming that participants retain
the unconditional right to withdraw at any juncture without penalty.
3. Data Privacy and Security: The system collects and processes sensitive
affective indices, behavioral patterns, and personal medical records. Strict data
protection mechanisms are implemented, encompassing AES-256 database
encryption at rest, TLS 1.3 encryption in transit, strict role-based access
restrictions, and automated data anonymization.
4. Mitigating Bias and Misinterpretation in AI Models: Affective computer
vision models trained solely on young adult populations reflect bias when
evaluated against elderly faces exhibiting deep facial wrinkles or age-related
muscular laxity. To eliminate misinterpretation (such as falsely classifying
neutral facial laxity as acute sadness or depression), the facial engine
incorporates baseline calibration and fuses vocal acoustic features to cross-
validate emotional state assessments.
5. Psychological Impact of Monitoring: Pervasive monitoring can induce
paranoia or agitation among vulnerable seniors. CareConnect was designed to
function discreetly during brief, voluntary 5-second wellness interactions,
avoiding any sense of persistent punitive surveillance.

PAGE 56
6. Responsibility and Human Oversight: The system strictly enforces a
"human-in-the-loop" paradigm. Machine learning outputs—whether extracted
prescription medications or detected emotional distress scores—serve purely
as assistive decision-support tools. Caretakers retain mandatory verification
authority, with explicit capability to override alerts or correct transcribed
dosages.
7. Ethical Use of Health Data: Medical records and prescription archives are
utilized strictly for direct resident care and authorized clinical evaluation. No
secondary data sharing, commercial monetization, or unapproved transfer
occurs, in full alignment with the Sri Lanka Personal Data Protection Act No.
9 of 2022 and Horizon Campus institutional ethics board regulations.
3.8 Methodology for the System Development
3.8.1 Agile Scrum Framework Selection Justification
Selecting an appropriate software development methodology is critical for
balancing complex machine learning experimentation with full-stack systems
engineering. Traditional sequential models (such as the Waterfall lifecycle) mandate
frozen requirements upfront, rendering them fundamentally unsuitable for deep
learning projects where model convergence rates, OCR accuracy thresholds, and
clinical edge cases cannot be predicted prior to empirical prototyping [49].
Consequently, the Agile Scrum framework was selected as the optimal
development methodology for CareConnect. Scrum emphasizes iterative increments,
continuous testing, cross-functional collaboration, and rapid adaptability. Given that
CareConnect comprises four interconnected modules (UI/UX, Edge Emotion AI,
Prescription OCR Ensemble, and System Engine), Scrum enabled the team to divide
development into two-week sprints. Each sprint delivered a shippable increment,
allowing continuous integration, regular supervisor feedback reviews, and rapid
tuning of deep learning hyperparameters based on empirical evaluation.

4.2 System Design Process
4.2.1 Overall System Architecture
When conceptualizing the system architecture for CareConnect, three
competing architectural strategies were evaluated:
Alternative 1: Monolithic Server Architecture: Implementing the entire
application (UI serving, relational database queries, heavy deep learning
PyTorch inferences, and audio-video processing) within a single centralized
Python Django or monolithic Node.js application. While simple to deploy
initially, this model suffers from catastrophic scalability bottlenecks: CPU-
intensive transformer inference blocks the asynchronous event loop of API
endpoints, and streaming raw webcam video from multiple resident rooms to a
central server saturates network bandwidth and creates severe security
vulnerabilities.
Alternative 2: Fully Serverless Cloud Architecture: Decomposing all
application logic into cloud-managed micro-functions (e.g., AWS Lambda,

PAGE 62
Google Cloud Run) with cloud-hosted computer vision APIs. Although highly
elastic, this approach was eliminated due to recurring token and invocation
costs that are economically unsustainable for underfunded charitable elder
orphanages, alongside complete operational paralysis whenever local internet
service experiences disruption.
Selected Strategy: Decoupled Edge-Ensemble Architecture: CareConnect
adopts a hybrid, decoupled microservice-inspired architecture that partitions
workloads based on computational characteristics and privacy mandates. As
illustrated in Figure 4.1, the architecture is partitioned into three autonomous,
interoperable tiers:
1. Client Presentation & Edge AI Tier: Combines a high-performance
React 18 single-page application and Expo React Native mobile apps
with browser-level edge ML runtimes (face-api.js and Web Audio
API). High-bandwidth sensory data (facial video frames and vocal
audio buffers) are processed entirely inside client memory,
significantly reducing privacy exposure by keeping raw audiovisual
data on-device;
2. Application Services & Ensemble AI Tier: Features a stateless
Node.js / Express.js REST API server managing business logic and
database transactions, flanked by an isolated asynchronous Python
FastAPI microservice dedicated exclusively to running the fine-tuned
Swin-BART Donut transformer model, and integrated with Google
Gemini 2.5 Flash for multimodal cross-validation; and
3. Persistence & Infrastructure Tier: Consists of an ACID-compliant
MySQL 8.0 relational database maintaining strict relational integrity,
backed by Firebase Authentication for cryptographic JWT issuance
and Firebase Cloud Storage for encrypted prescription image
archiving.

4.4.2 Database Normalization (1NF, 2NF, 3NF Analysis)
To prevent update anomalies, insertion inconsistencies, and redundant data
storage, the relational schema was subjected to formal Third Normal Form (3NF)
normalization:
First Normal Form (1NF): All attributes contain strictly atomic, scalar
values, and every table possesses an explicit primary key. Multi-valued
repeating groups (such as multiple medications prescribed on a single paper
slip) were excised from the prescriptions table and decomposed into a
separate dependent medications relation where each tuple represents
exactly one atomic pharmaceutical item linked via foreign key.
Second Normal Form (2NF): The schema satisfies 1NF and guarantees that
all non-key attributes are fully functionally dependent on the entire primary
key, eliminating partial functional dependencies. In associative bridge tables
with composite primary keys (e.g., caretaker_residents where the
primary key is (caretaker_id, resident_id)), the non-key attribute
assigned_at depends strictly on the combination of both keys rather than
either key in isolation.
Third Normal Form (3NF): The schema satisfies 2NF and contains zero
transitive functional dependencies (X → Y and Y → Z). For instance, in earlier

PAGE 79
designs, the appointments table stored caretaker contact numbers directly
(appointment_id → caretaker_id → phone). Normalization removed
the transitive attribute, ensuring that caregiver contact details reside solely
within the caretakers relation, referenced only via foreign key.

5.5.3 Vocal Tone Feature Extraction Engine

export class VocalToneEngine {
  private calculateAcousticFeatures(timeDomain: Float32Array, freqData: Uint8A
rray): VocalFeatures {
    let sumSquares = 0;
    for (let i = 0; i < timeDomain.length; i++) sumSquares += timeDomain[i] * 
timeDomain[i];
    const rmsEnergy = Math.sqrt(sumSquares / timeDomain.length);
    const pitchHz = this.autoCorrelate(timeDomain, this.audioContext.sampleRat
e);
    let num = 0, den = 0;
    for (let i = 0; i < freqData.length; i++) { num += i * freqData[i]; den += 
freqData[i]; }
    const spectralCentroid = den > 0 ? num / den : 0;
    return { rmsEnergy, pitchHz, spectralCentroid };
  }
}

imperil human life. This chapter presents an exhaustive, multi-dimensional testing
regimen executed across the CareConnect ecosystem. It articulates the testing
methodologies deployed, presents structured test cases across functional and security
domains, documents quantitative empirical benchmarks for both machine learning
pipelines (Prescription OCR Ensemble and Multimodal Emotion Detection), provides
backend server stress metrics, and evaluates operational feasibility through User
Acceptance Testing (UAT) utilizing the standardized System Usability Scale (SUS).
6.2 Testing Procedure
Testing was executed systematically across five rigorous tiers:
1. Unit Testing: Testing individual functions, algorithms, and classes in
isolation. This included validating regular expression tokenizers in
MedicationParser.ts, Levenshtein distance calculations in
rxnormDictionary.ts, audio FFT autocorrelation in
VocalToneEngine.ts, and parameterized SQL queries in the Node.js
database layer using Jest and Vitest frameworks;
2. Integration Testing: Verifying communication protocols and data
serialization between decoupled microservices. Key test suites validated the
HTTP multipart payload transfer between the React client and the FastAPI
Donut container, the asynchronous promise resolution within
EnsembleOrchestrator, and JWT authorization headers between
Express.js and Firebase Admin SDK;
3. System Testing: End-to-end execution of complete user workflows within a
staging environment replicating residential care conditions, evaluating

PAGE 94
prescription upload to medication scheduling, emotion capture to emergency
alert dispatch, and staff leave request to resident reassignment;
4. Performance & Stress Testing: Benchmarking inference latency of deep
learning models under varied hardware conditions, evaluating edge browser
frame rates (FPS), and stress-testing the Express.js / MySQL backend under
simulated concurrent user traffic using Apache JMeter and Autocannon; and
5. Security & Penetration Testing: Auditing authentication boundaries,
verifying that unauthenticated requests to protected endpoints return HTTP
401 Unauthorized, validating that caretakers cannot access administrative
diagnostics (HTTP 403 Forbidden), and verifying SQL injection immunity
through automated penetration test scripts.

7.1 Project Summary
The accelerating demographic aging of South Asia, specifically within Sri
Lanka, has placed an unprecedented burden upon charitable elder orphanages and
residential geriatric care facilities. Operating under chronic financial limitations, these
institutions struggle with severe caregiver shortages, paper-dependent medical
recordkeeping, unmonitored medication adherence, and an inability to detect
emerging emotional or physical distress among vulnerable residents. This research
conceived, engineered, and empirically validated CareConnect—an intelligent,
privacy-preserving healthcare management and ambient monitoring ecosystem
designed specifically for resource-constrained institutional eldercare environments.
The system addresses these issues by integrating two machine learning
components within a three-tier web and mobile architecture:
1. An Asynchronous Ensemble Document Understanding Pipeline: Combines
a fine-tuned Swin-BART vision transformer (Donut model), Google Gemini
2.5 Flash multimodal reasoning, and deterministic RxNorm pharmacological
lookups via confidence-weighted voting, transforming illegible handwritten
doctor prescriptions into structured, verified medication schedules; and
2. An On-Device Edge Multimodal Affective Telemetry Engine: Orchestrates
client-side facial landmark geometry tracking (face-api.js) and acoustic vocal
tone frequency-energy profiling (Web Audio API) directly within the user's
web browser, executing continuous wellness assessment without transmitting
raw video or audio buffers across network boundaries.
Supported by a stateless Node.js / Express.js REST application layer, an
ACID-compliant MySQL relational database, and responsive clients across React 18
web portals and Expo React Native mobile applications, CareConnect provides role-
tailored tooling for Patients, Caretakers, and Administrators. Rigorous empirical
benchmarking demonstrated that the ensemble OCR achieved an F1-score of 0.932
(CER: 0.038), the edge emotion engine attained 88.5% classification accuracy with
sub-120ms latency, and User Acceptance Testing yielded an outstanding System
Usability Scale (SUS) score of 85.1 across 24 system stakeholders.

7.3 Answers to Research Questions
The four research questions defined in the approved Research Proposal
(Section 1.3) are answered below using only the empirical evidence generated during
this study.
Research Question 1: How accurately can a computer vision-based model
extract and interpret handwritten or printed prescriptions to ensure correct
medication identification and dosage schedules?

PAGE 115
The proposed ensemble OCR pipeline — integrating a fine-tuned Swin-BART
Document Understanding Transformer (Donut), Google Gemini 2.5 Flash multimodal
reasoning, and RxNorm pharmacological dictionary look-ups through confidence-
weighted voting — achieved an F1-score of 0.932 and a Character Error Rate (CER)
of 0.038 on the evaluated English clinical prescription dataset. These metrics
demonstrate that the system can reliably extract medication names, dosage quantities,
and frequency instructions from handwritten clinical scripts. However, the system
retains a mandatory human-in-the-loop verification step before clinical medication
dispensation to safeguard patient safety.
Research Question 2: Which OCR and NLP techniques are most effective for
prescription analysis in diverse handwriting formats and multi-language
(Tamil/Sinhala/English) contexts?
The Donut + Gemini + RxNorm ensemble demonstrated strong effectiveness
for English-language handwritten prescription interpretation, significantly
outperforming solitary models (Donut alone: F1 = 0.829; Gemini alone: F1 = 0.876)
by +12.5% in F1-score. Confidence-weighted voting proved the most robust fusion
strategy for resolving disagreements between heterogeneous OCR engines. However,
handwritten Sinhala and Tamil prescription recognition was not evaluated in this
study due to the absence of annotated vernacular medical handwriting datasets.
Therefore, RQ2 was partially addressed — the ensemble architecture is validated for
English scripts, while multilingual extension remains an identified direction for future
research.
Research Question 3: What are the challenges in integrating a smart
prescription reader with real-time patient alert systems in underfunded orphanages
and elderly care facilities?
The development and deployment process identified the following key
integration challenges: (a) network dependency — the cloud-based Gemini
verification step requires internet connectivity, necessitating graceful offline fallback
to local Donut and regex parsing; (b) low-resource hardware constraints — fine-
tuning vision transformers demands GPU resources typically unavailable in care
facilities, requiring cloud-based model training with local edge inference; (c) privacy
requirements — continuous affective monitoring must not transmit raw biometric
data, mandating an edge-first processing architecture; (d) environmental sensitivity
— facial landmark detection degrades in poor lighting and vocal tone extraction is
affected by ambient institutional noise; (e) system integration complexity —
coordinating asynchronous microservices (Donut, Gemini, Node.js API) required
robust fault tolerance via Promise.allSettled; and (f) human oversight

PAGE 116
necessity — AI-generated prescription outputs must be verified by caretakers before
clinical dispensation to prevent medication errors.
Research Question 4: Can the integration of prescription reading with
dietary and emotional alert modules enhance medication adherence and overall
patient care in low-resource caregiving environments?
CareConnect successfully integrates automated prescription reminders,
personalized dietary notifications, continuous emotional wellness monitoring, SOS
emergency alerts, and centralized caretaker management functions into a unified
platform. These mechanisms provide the technical infrastructure that supports
medication adherence and coordinated patient care by ensuring timely medication
reminders are generated from digitized prescriptions, dietary contra-indications are
flagged automatically, and emotional distress alerts are dispatched to on-duty
caregivers in real time. User Acceptance Testing confirmed high operational
feasibility (SUS score: 85.1) and positive qualitative feedback from stakeholders.
However, the study did not conduct a long-term controlled trial or longitudinal field
evaluation to conclusively demonstrate sustained improvement in real-world
medication adherence rates or overall health outcomes. Therefore, while the system
demonstrably provides the mechanisms to enhance adherence and care, definitive
evidence of sustained real-world improvement requires future longitudinal evaluation.

Empirical Validation of Ensemble OCR for Cursive Clinical Scripts:

WebAssembly and WebGL, the system generates vital clinical distress alerts
without transmitting raw video or audio data across networks.
Holistic Operational Ecosystem for Low-Resource Institutions: Unlike
narrow point solutions, CareConnect provides an end-to-end management
platform uniting medication scheduling, adherence auditing, emergency alert
triage, staff duty leave management, and AI-tailored nutritional planning
within an accessible, low-overhead software footprint.

PAGE 118
Application of Health Technology in Resource-Limited Settings: Built
entirely on open-source frameworks and commodity consumer devices,
demonstrating that advanced artificial intelligence can be deployed in
resource-limited eldercare institutions in developing nations without
prohibitive licensing or hardware costs.
7.5 Lessons Learned and Engineering Challenges
The execution of this project provided critical engineering and
interdisciplinary insights:
Managing High GPU VRAM Demands in Vision Transformers: Fine-
tuning the Donut model on a Tesla T4 GPU initially triggered severe CUDA
Out-of-Memory (OOM) errors during backpropagation. The challenge was
resolved by reducing the batch size to 1, activating gradient accumulation
across 8 steps (yielding an effective batch size of 8), and enabling PyTorch
gradient checkpointing to trade minor computational overhead for a 40%
reduction in peak VRAM consumption.
Heterogeneous Acoustic Environmental Noise: Initial vocal tone algorithms
produced noisy pitch estimates in reverberant institutional spaces. This was
mitigated by implementing time-domain auto-correlation with parabolic
interpolation and applying an energy threshold filter to discard silent or
background-noise buffers below -42 dBFS.
Designing for Geriatric Visual and Cognitive Declines: Initial UI prototypes
featuring compact data tables and subtle iconography proved confusing for
elderly residents. Re-engineering the patient interface to emphasize high-
contrast color cards, large typography (16 pt or larger body text), multi-
sensory status indicators, and one-touch actions dramatically elevated user
comprehension and satisfaction.
Asynchronous Multi-Service Fault Tolerance: Coordinating parallel
microservices (Donut, Gemini, Node API) required robust exception handling.
Utilizing Promise.allSettled in the client orchestrator ensured that
temporary API timeouts in one engine never caused catastrophic client
crashes, allowing graceful degradation to available local models.
7.6 Limitations of the Project
Notwithstanding its achievements, several technical limitations must be
acknowledged:

PAGE 119
Physical Paper Damage Boundaries: Severely damaged paper prescriptions
—exhibiting heavy oil stains, torn text lines, or severe ink bleeding—remain
beyond reliable machine transcription, requiring mandatory caretaker visual
confirmation.
Vernacular Script Constraints: The OCR and RxNorm pipelines are
currently optimized for English medical terminology, Latin dosage notations,
and international brand/generic pharmaceuticals. Vernacular Sinhala and Tamil
medical notations are not yet supported.
Ambient Lighting and Occlusion Dependencies: In dim nocturnal ward
lighting or when a resident wears an opaque face mask or turns away from the
webcam, facial emotion confidence degrades, shifting the affective engine to
rely primarily on vocal tone.
Absence of Invasive Hemodynamic Vitals: The system captures non-
invasive affective telemetry (facial expression and vocal tone) and does not
measure direct hemodynamic parameters (such as blood glucose or invasive
blood pressure), which require dedicated diagnostic medical devices.
Absence of Longitudinal Field Evaluation: The system was validated
through technical benchmarks and a cross-sectional User Acceptance Testing
session. A long-term longitudinal deployment measuring sustained changes in
medication adherence, caretaker response time, and resident health outcomes
was not conducted within the project timeframe.
7.7 Recommendations for Future Work
Based on the empirical findings, actionable recommendations are formulated
across three target sectors:
7.7.1 Recommendations for Clinical Practitioners and Care Home Directors
Adopt Human-in-the-Loop Verification: Institutional caregivers should
utilize AI prescription digitization as an augmenting assistive tool, maintaining
mandatory human visual sign-off on extracted medications before clinical
dispensation.
Incorporate Regular Edge Wellness Checks: Establish scheduled 5-second
wellness checks into resident morning and evening routines, utilizing the
longitudinal affective graphs to detect early signs of clinical depression or
medication side-effects.

PAGE 120
7.7.2 Recommendations for Healthcare Policymakers and Regulatory Authorities
Standardize Edge Privacy Frameworks in Geriatric Care: Regulatory
bodies should formally mandate edge-first data processing standards for
institutional eldercare surveillance, safeguarding personal dignity and ensuring
compliance with national data privacy statutes.
Subsidize Digital Healthcare Tools for Non-Profit Care Homes:
Government ministries of health and social services should allocate grant
funding to equip charitable elder homes with basic computing hardware and
local Wi-Fi infrastructure to support digital health ecosystems.
7.7.3 Recommendations for Future Researchers and Industry Engineers
Multilingual Prescription Recognition: Extend the Donut training pipeline
to incorporate multi-script handwritten datasets, supporting vernacular Sinhala
and Tamil clinical handwriting alongside English.
Cross-Modal Pharmacovigilance: Integrate an automated drug-drug
interaction (DDI) engine into the prescription ensemble that automatically
evaluates the resident's active medical history and cross-checks for adverse
pharmacological contraindications.
In-Bed Infrared Micro-Sensing: Explore non-contact thermal or infrared
optical sensors capable of estimating respiratory rates and resting heart rates
during sleep, augmenting daytime affective telemetry with continuous
nocturnal vital sign tracking.
Longitudinal Controlled Deployment Study: Conduct a multi-month
before-and-after field trial at partner care facilities to measure the real-world
impact of CareConnect on medication adherence rates, caretaker response
latency, and resident health outcomes, providing the definitive evidence
needed to validate the full scope of the research objectives.
7.8 Concluding Remarks
CareConnect demonstrates that artificial intelligence, transformer-based
document intelligence, and privacy-preserving affective computing can be
harmonized into an accessible, robust, and economically viable healthcare
management platform for underfunded geriatric institutions. By automating the
transcription of complex handwritten prescriptions, capturing unobtrusive emotional
wellness telemetry, and streamlining fragmented administrative workflows, the
system bridges the critical operational divide between elderly residents, human

caregivers, and healthcare administrators. As societies worldwide confront the
immense challenges of demographic aging, CareConnect stands as an academically
defensible, technologically validated, and deeply human-centered contribution toward
dignified, compassionate, and intelligent geriatric care.
