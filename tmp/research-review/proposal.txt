As the global elderly population rises, especially in countries like Sri Lanka, the need for 
intelligent caregiving solutions has become increasingly urgent. Traditional care models in 
elderly orphanages are often under-resourced and lack the capacity for continuous 
monitoring, which leads to delayed responses to health or emotional issues. The integration 
of Artificial Intelligence (AI) into elder care presents a transformative opportunity to

enhance resident safety, provide timely interventions, and improve the overall quality of 
life. 
AI-Based Monitoring Systems have been widely researched for patient and elderly care. A 
study by Chen et al. (2020) explored how AI-powered vision systems can monitor physical 
movements to detect falls, wandering, or abnormal behaviors among the elderly. This 
supports your project’s core component of behavior tracking through camera-based 
systems. Similarly, the work of Nasrallah et al. (2021) demonstrates how smart cameras 
with object detection algorithms can interpret daily activities in assisted living facilities, 
contributing to reduced risks of unattended incidents. 
Emotion Detection through Computer Vision has gained attention in mental health 
monitoring. Ekman’s (1992) foundational theory on facial expressions has laid the 
groundwork for modern AI models to interpret emotional states. More recent work by Li & 
Deng (2021) shows that deep learning techniques such as CNN and LSTM can classify 
emotions in real time based on facial data. This is highly relevant to your objective of 
detecting emotional distress in elderly residents. 
Medical Alert Systems and Health Monitoring have evolved significantly in recent years. 
A study by Ahmed et al. (2019) proposed an IoT-based health monitoring system using 
wearable sensors to track vitals like heart rate and body temperature. While your project 
replaces wearables with visual and data-driven detection, the principle of real-time health 
alerts remains foundational. Furthermore, integrating Electronic Health Records (EHR) to 
create personalized medication alerts has been examined by Kruse et al. (2018), 
highlighting the need for automation in medication tracking and compliance. 
Another dimension your project touches is Ethical AI and Privacy in Surveillance, 
especially in vulnerable populations. A survey by Bhattacharya et al. (2022) on privacy-
aware smart health systems emphasizes the importance of anonymized data handling and 
responsible alert mechanisms when using surveillance in healthcare environments. 
Moreover, alert and notification mechanisms have been tested in multiple smart home 
environments. Research by Kapoor et al. (2020) found that real-time alerts integrated with 
mobile applications increased caregiver response rates by over 30%. This aligns with your 
system’s real-time response component, aimed at enhancing the efficiency of caretakers in 
understaffed elderly homes. 
Despite the promising advancements, there is limited research combining all these 
elements—AI-based behavior detection, emotional state analysis, personalized health 
tracking, and alert generation—into a single, unified system tailored for resource-limited 
elderly orphanages. This gap confirms the necessity of your project, which aims to bring 
together these technologies into a practical, context-specific solution for Sri Lanka’s 
elderly care institutions.

Do you foresee any ethical issues with your research? If yes, please explain clearly. Ethical 
approval will be needed for any research involving human and animal participants. Research 
that raises ethical issues such as environmental damage or political and social tensions will

Yes, there are several ethical issues that need to be carefully addressed in this research and 
development project, particularly because it involves AI-driven monitoring of elderly 
individuals and the handling of sensitive personal data. These ethical considerations 
include: 
 
1. Privacy and Surveillance Concerns 
The use of camera-based AI systems raises concerns about constant surveillance in care 
environments. Elderly individuals may feel discomfort or loss of dignity due to being 
monitored. The system must prioritize non-intrusive monitoring and clearly define what 
data is collected and how it is used. Ensuring the system respects the residents’ personal 
space and autonomy is crucial. 
 
2. Informed Consent and Participant Rights 
Elderly residents (or their legal guardians) must give informed, voluntary consent before 
any monitoring occurs. They should be made fully aware of the purpose of the system, 
what data is being collected (visual, emotional, health-related), and how it will be stored 
and used. Consent forms and verbal explanations must be provided in clear, understandable 
language. 
 
3. Data Privacy and Security 
The system will collect and process emotion data, behavior patterns, and medical details, 
which are extremely sensitive. It is essential to implement strong data protection strategies 
such as: 
• Encrypted data storage 
• Restricted access controls 
• No unnecessary data sharing 
Additionally, data should be anonymized where possible, and deleted securely 
when no longer needed. 
 
4. Bias and Misinterpretation in AI Models 

PAGE 8

AI models used to detect emotions or behaviors may reflect bias if not properly trained or 
validated, especially with age-specific expressions or cultural differences. 
Misinterpretation (e.g., falsely classifying sadness or distress) could lead to inaccurate 
alerts or responses. The system must be trained on diverse, age-appropriate datasets and 
regularly audited to minimize these risks. 
 
5. Psychological Impact of Monitoring 
Continuous monitoring could lead to emotional discomfort or stress among elderly 
residents, especially if they are aware of being watched. The system must be designed to 
function discreetly, avoiding any sense of being under surveillance, and feedback should be 
collected from residents to assess their comfort level. 
 
6. Responsibility and Human Oversight 
AI systems must not operate without human-in-the-loop supervision. Caretakers should 
have the ability to verify alerts, override false positives, and interpret outputs responsibly. 
Clear roles must be established to define who is accountable for decisions or actions based 
on system alerts. 
 
7. Ethical Use of Health Data 
Medical data should only be used for the purposes stated in the project proposal. No 
secondary use (e.g., for research or training) should occur without additional consent. The 
project must follow national health data protection laws and university ethical guidelines. 
 
Summary 
This project involves human participants (elderly residents) and the use of AI systems to 
monitor sensitive behavior and health data, thus requiring ethical approval from the 
university's ethics review board. The research will follow a comprehensive ethical 
framework addressing consent, data protection, fairness, transparency, and human dignity, 
ensuring the system provides value without causing harm, bias, or discomfort to the 
residents it is designed to protect.
